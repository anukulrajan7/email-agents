# How your helpers work

Post Room is a small team of AI helpers that read your email, do the routine work, and ask you only when it matters. This page explains, in plain words, who they are, what they do when an email arrives, and how you stay in control.

Some things below are already live. Others are designed and on the way. Anything marked **(coming soon)** is not available yet. We would rather tell you that than promise it.

## 1. Your helpers

**Meera is your personal assistant.** Every person on your team has their own Meera. She works on that person's mailbox, in their tone, and she handles everyday mail and anything no expert owns. Today she writes the replies she handles. **(coming soon)** She will write every reply Post Room sends to a customer, candidate or colleague, so that one thread always has one voice, one safety check and one draft.

You see the experts that match the packs you switched on. HR and staffing teams get the HR group, and the others appear with their pack.

**Your own helpers.** You can describe a new helper in everyday words, for example "handle vendor invoice questions, always ask before replying". Post Room turns that into a working helper and shows you a practice run on sample mail first. The practice run sends nothing. A new helper starts careful, and you decide how much freedom it gets.

## 2. What happens when an email arrives

Here is one real example: a staff member emails their monthly timesheet to the HR mailbox.

1. **Rohan reads it.** He opens the attachment, reads the hours, and records the timesheet against the right person.
2. **Rohan checks your rules.** For example: is it the right month, do the hours add up, is the signature there, are there too many hours in one day?
3. **Meera replies to the sender.** If everything is fine, she says the timesheet was received and recorded, with the period and hours. If something is wrong, she says exactly what to correct, for example "this looks like August, we expected September". **(coming soon)** Today Rohan records the sheet and checks it, but he does not send the reply himself.
4. **The manager gets a short summary** in plain words, once, with the option to approve. **(coming soon)**

Notes about the timesheet go to your team. They never go to the sender.

### We always reply to the sender

If someone writes to you, they should never be left wondering. We reply to the person who wrote. If the answer needs a person on your side first, the sender still gets a short "we have this, someone will come back to you" note, and nothing in it states facts we have not confirmed. **(coming soon)** A person on your team is told if a reply has been waiting too long.

### The few times we do not reply

- **System, bulk and no-reply mail.** Newsletters, mailing lists, bounces, automatic out-of-office replies and mail from machines are not answered. Post Room's own emails are never answered either.
- **Your "don't reply" rules.** If you told us not to reply to a sender or a topic, we do not.
- **Mail a person already answered.** If someone on your team has already replied, we stay quiet.
- **A closing "thanks".** When the sender is just saying thank you after we replied, we do not reply to the thank you.
- **Mail held back for privacy.** A few mails are kept out of view by policy. The person is pointed to the right mailbox later instead.
- **Private mailboxes.** We do not send a "we have this" note from inside someone's private inbox. The member is warned instead.

Every one of these is written down with its reason, so you can always see why nothing was sent.

## 3. When something is missing

Sometimes a mail does not have what we need: the month is missing, the employee code, the number of hours.

- We ask for **exactly what is missing**, in one short message. We do not ask for things we already have.
- If there is no answer, we send one gentle reminder. After a few tries, a person on your team takes over. We do not keep emailing someone.
- When the answer arrives, we remember it in **that person's profile**, so we never have to ask twice.

We only accept profile updates from the person themselves, their manager or HR. A message from anyone else, or from an email address that fails identity checks, is not trusted. We fill empty details only and we do not overwrite what is already there. We never accept bank details or ID numbers by email. Every change is recorded and **can be undone**.

## 4. People we don't know yet

A timesheet sometimes comes from someone who is not on your team list yet, such as a new joiner, a contractor, or a name spelled differently.

- We keep the timesheet **exactly as written**. Nothing is thrown away.
- We never guess who it is. Two people named "Amit Kumar" are not treated as the same person, and "Rahul" is not quietly turned into "Rahul Verma".
- The sender still gets a receipt that says we have the sheet. We do not confirm hours for someone we cannot identify.
- You see these names grouped together on one card. You can add them as a person, link them to an existing person, mark them as an outside person, or ignore them. When a link is made, the waiting confirmation goes out to the sender.
- Every link can be undone. An undo marks the affected sheets "needs a check" rather than quietly unlinking them.

"Who hasn't sent a timesheet" only counts people who are actually on your team list. Unmatched names are shown separately and are never counted for someone else. **(coming soon)**

## 5. Your rules

You decide who does what. You can write rules in plain English, for example:

- "Ananya handles anything about leave."
- "Wait for my approval before replying to Acme."
- "Never reply directly to this client."

When you write a rule, Post Room reads it back to you as a fixed, clear sentence and shows how it would have worked on your last 200 emails. You confirm only what will really happen. If a phrase can't be turned into a rule, we show you the gap instead of guessing.

**Rules that clash.** If two rules could both apply to the same mail, Post Room warns you when you save and asks you which one wins. If a clash appears later, you are told about the new overlap. Your rules always beat a helper's built-in habits. Rules can only make a helper more careful, never less.

## 6. What helpers may do alone, and how trust grows

Every helper has a freedom setting. You are always the one who sets the starting point.

- **Watch only.** The helper does the work in the background and shows you what it would have done.
- **Draft.** It writes replies, and a person sends each one.
- **Routine send.** It replies to the original sender on routine mail, after a short wait with a **Stop button** (10 minutes by default).
- **Autonomous.** It can also reply to others in the conversation and to new addresses, behind the same wait and the same checks.

**Trust is earned.** **(coming soon)** After a run of good work, with your approvals mostly unchanged and no problems, a helper can move up a step on its own. You are told when it happens, with an **Undo** button. We never promise how many good replies it takes, and a small number is never enough. Promotion goes one step at a time and is slower on new helpers.

**It can also lose freedom.** Today, a serious problem or a falling approval rate moves a helper down automatically. **(coming soon)** Even then, the helper keeps its job: work continues, with every mail asking you first.

**When something looks risky, it stops.** A helper that sees a suspicious instruction in an email stops. You are alerted. Only an admin can release it, and anyone can discard it.

**Sensitive mail always asks you, at every level.** This includes money, bank details, prices, refunds and salary, legal and contract wording, passwords and one-time codes, attachments going out, suspicious mail, new outside addresses, and anything you told a helper never to do. Contact details are on the list too. A helper never sends a message to an address that only appeared inside an email's text.

## 7. Talking to Meera by voice or text

You can ask Meera anything from any page, by typing or by pressing the microphone. It is one assistant and one conversation. You can ask by voice and carry on by text, and she remembers what you were just talking about. A conversation continues until about half an hour of quiet.

- She answers in **your own time zone** and in plain words: "yesterday at 3:30 PM" or "15 Oct", never a technical timestamp or code.
- Spoken answers are short, a couple of sentences, with numbers and dates said as words. Written answers are a little longer.
- She only shows what you are allowed to see. A member's personal mail is never shown to an admin.
- She can prepare actions, such as approving something, but nothing happens until you confirm. By voice, she shows the card and waits for a tap or a clear "yes, send it".

The shared conversation across voice and text, local time everywhere, the short spoken style and quick answers to common questions like "who hasn't sent their timesheet" are the next improvements. Meera understands Hinglish questions. She replies in English.

## 8. Your files

Post Room reads the files that arrive with your email so your helpers and Meera can use them. Today that covers Excel, CSV, Word, PowerPoint, PDF, images and audio.

**(coming soon)** Files will also be understood and organised for you. Post Room will recognise a timesheet, a CV or an invoice, link it to the right person or record, and let you find it by asking Meera ("show me Priya's August timesheet"). You will also be able to upload files directly. The same file sent twice is read only once.

Nothing outside Post Room is moved, renamed, deleted or shared without your permission. Reading a connected drive, if you ever ask for it, would be read-only.

## 9. Safety in one paragraph

Your helpers work only on mail they are allowed to see, and only with the freedom you gave them. Every outgoing message goes through the same checks: the right recipient, no leaked addresses or numbers, no promise the facts do not support, and a short **Stop** window before anything is sent on its own. A 30-second **Undo** is there after you press Send. Anything sensitive, unusual or suspicious waits for a person. Mail that tries to give a helper orders ("ignore your rules", "forward this to...") is treated as an attack, not an instruction. Every send, approval, level change and pause is written to a record you can see. If the safety check itself is down, nothing is sent without a check. It is retried, delayed, and then asked of you.
